Rules

Fuseki Shogi is a shogi variant in which both players place 20 pieces on an empty board, one at a time, before the game begins. Balance Shogi adds a short opening procedure that lets the players weigh the first-move advantage against king safety themselves.

Fuseki Shogi

  1. The board starts empty. Starting with Sente, the players alternately place their 20 pieces (9 pawns, 2 lances, 2 knights, 2 silvers, 2 golds, 1 bishop, 1 rook, 1 king) within their own first four ranks.
  2. Until both players have placed all 20 pieces (40 moves), nothing moves. Only placements.
  3. To avoid a double pawn (nifu), filling all four squares of a file with your own non-pawn pieces is illegal. All nine pawns must end up on nine different files, so filling a file with other pieces would force a double pawn somewhere else. The rule stops it one move early.
  4. From move 41 it is ordinary shogi. Sente moves first. Checkmate wins.

The move-41 adjudication and the move-40 restriction

Check and checkmate are not considered during placement, so after 40 placements a king may already be attacked. This site handles it as follows.

  • Adjudication at move 41: if the side to move (Sente) could capture the opposing king after the 40th placement, Sente wins.
  • Restriction on move 40: Gote’s last placement may not leave Gote’s own king attacked. The only exception is when Gote’s king was already in check after move 39 and one piece cannot cover it; then the restriction is lifted and the adjudication decides.

Sente’s last placement (move 39) has no such restriction. Sente being in check at move 41 is an ordinary check, not an illegal position.

Balance Shogi

Fuseki Shogi favours Sente: between AIs Sente wins a little over 60%, and the gap widens as the engines get stronger. Balance Shogi weighs that first-move advantage against king safety. One player places both kings; the other decides between moving first with the thinner king and moving second with the sturdier one. It is the pie rule (one cuts, the other chooses) applied to the kings, so the balance holds at whatever strength the players have.

Procedure

  1. Furigoma decides who places the kings. The other player chooses the side.
  2. The placer puts the Sente king somewhere in Sente’s camp (the first four ranks) — move 1.
  3. The same player then puts the Gote king in Gote’s camp — move 2.
  4. The other player declares whether they play Sente or Gote. This fixes the sides for the game. The position does not change.
  5. The remaining 19 pieces per side are placed alternately, Sente first (moves 3–40). The kings are already on the board.
  6. After Gote’s 40th placement the placement ends. Sente plays move 41 and the game continues as ordinary shogi.

The placement zone, the nifu-avoidance restriction, the move-40 restriction and the move-41 adjudication are unchanged. Only the first two moves and the choice are new.

Who places, and who is Sente

Who places the kings is decided by furigoma; the sides (Sente, Gote) are decided by the choice. The placer is not necessarily Sente. The game record keeps both.

Why it balances

On one pan of the scale sits the first move, on the other the gap in king safety. Kings of equal safety leave the first move unpaid, so the chooser takes Sente; too thin a Sente king and the chooser takes Gote. The placer breaks even only when the gap in safety matches the first move, and that judgement is made by the placer at their own strength. No measurement and no numeric compensation is needed. Read more in How Balance Shogi came to be.

What the AI does

On this site the AI consults a table of Sente’s winning rate for every pair of king squares, both when placing and when choosing. When placing it draws a random pair from those closest to 50%; when choosing it takes Sente whenever the table value exceeds 50%. From move 3 the placement engine continues as usual. There is no search.

Game record format

A game is written as USI drops (such as P*7f) separated by spaces. In Balance Shogi, one extra token follows move 2: choose:sente or choose:gote. Paste a sequence under “Game record” in the menu to replay it.

SFEN during placement is a Fuseki Shogi extension with kings in hand and cannot be read by ordinary shogi software. From move 41 it is standard SFEN.
